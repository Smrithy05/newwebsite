About the SSUS

Established in 1993 in Kalady, Kerala—the birthplace of the philosopher Adi Shankara—the Sree Sankaracharya University of Sanskrit (SSUS) is a premier public institution dedicated to advancing higher learning in Sanskrit, Indian philosophy, and classical heritage. Accredited with an "A+" grade by the NAAC, the university bridges traditional Vedic and textual scholarship with contemporary education by offering undergraduate, postgraduate, and research programs across humanities, social sciences, fine arts, and modern languages. Operating through its main campus along the banks of the Periyar River and multiple regional centres across Kerala, SSUS plays a pivotal role in democratizing access to higher education, fostering intercultural dialogue, and preserving India’s intellectual and artistic traditions.

Department of Geography

Established in 1994 under the Faculty of Arts and Social Sciences at the Kalady campus, the Department of Geography at Sree Sankaracharya University of Sanskrit (SSUS) is a prominent center for geographical education and spatial research in Kerala. Offering postgraduate and doctoral programs—including its flagship M.Sc. in Geography and collaborative multidisciplinary courses in Disaster Management—the department integrates classical physical and human geography with modern geoinformatics, remote sensing, and GIS applications. Through rigorous fieldwork, socioeconomic surveys, and community-level resource mapping initiatives, it trains students to address real-world environmental and developmental challenges, preparing graduates for key roles in spatial analysis, urban planning, environmental consultancy, and academic research.

About WebGIS

WebGIS for Aranmula Grama Panchayat serves as a vital digital spatial platform designed to modernize decentralized governance, natural resource management, and disaster risk reduction within the ecologically sensitive Pamba River basin. By integrating high-resolution geographic data, cadastral boundaries, land-use classifications, and infrastructure networks into an interactive web-based interface, the system enables local administrators, planners, and citizens to visualize ward-level assets and demographic patterns in real time. Given Aranmula’s vulnerability to monsoon flooding and its status as a critical cultural heritage zone, the WebGIS application plays an indispensable role in flood inundation modeling, evacuation planning, wetland conservation, and transparent participatory planning for local self-government development schemes.
